Video Game Collections

Exploring platforms, genres, publishers and release years across gaming eras

Video games are a popular form of entertainment played on computers, consoles and mobile devices. They cover many genres, including adventure, sports, racing and role-playing, and can be played alone or with others.

Over time, advances in technology have changed how games are developed, distributed and played.

Section 1

How Have Video Games Changed Over Time?

Digital stores gave smaller developers more ways to publish games without relying on shop shelves. Affordable development tools and growing mobile audiences also opened opportunities for new creators.

During this period, recorded releases in the IGDB dataset grew from 2,750 in 2010 to 18,108 in 2024—a 6.6-fold increase.

Adventure’s share nearly doubled, rising from 20.9% in 2010 to 38.4% in 2024—the largest increase among the selected genres. Action remained the most common, accounting for 40.2% of recorded main-game releases in 2024. Action games range from Elden Ring’s open‑world combat to Hades’ repeated escape challenges.

Puzzle showed the largest decrease in share, falling from 16.8% to 9.8%. Its recorded game count still increased, but the overall collection grew faster.

Horror rose from fourth to first, overtaking Fantasy in 2023. Recorded releases grew from 335 to 1,433 by 2024. Shorter horror games can suit small development teams, while streamers’ reactions help them reach wider audiences.

Erotic made the largest climb, moving from seventh to third. Its rise coincided with Steam’s broader content policy and adult-content filters introduced in 2018. Meanwhile, Science fiction fell from second to fourth, despite recorded releases increasing from 546 to 734.

Section 2

How Are Video Games Designed to Be Played?

Early games were mostly flat: in 1980–1984, 43.7% of games with a recorded perspective used a side view. By 2020–2024, first-person games had more than doubled their share, and virtual reality appeared at 5%.

The two periods are very different in size (2,294 games compared with 24,142), so the chart compares shares rather than counts.

80% of multiplayer-capable games also support single player, giving players the choice to play alone or with others. Sackboy: A Big Adventure, for example, offers solo adventures and co-operative play with up to four players.

Co-operative play appears in 9,929 games, compared with 3,053 offering split screen. Co-op focuses on working together towards shared goals. It Takes Two builds its adventure around two players helping each other overcome challenges, either on the couch or online.

Section 3

How Do Game Libraries Differ Across Platforms?

Each rectangle counts the recorded games listed on a platform, not sales. A game released on several platforms is counted once for each.

PC dominates: Windows, Mac and Linux together hold about 60% of the selected platform listings, partly because computers are open to small studios and solo developers. Nintendo Switch has the most listings of any console.

Age ratings show who a game is made for. Under the North American ESRB system, E is for everyone, T for ages 13 and over, and M for ages 17 and over.

Sports and Racing games flow mostly to E, Role-Playing mostly to T, and Shooter is the only genre where M is the largest rating. Action spreads across all four ratings.

Section 4

Who Creates and Publishes Our Games?

Wider columns mean larger catalogues across the six genres. Sega has the biggest.

Each publisher has its own speciality: Konami leans heavily on Sports, Microsoft on Action and Shooter, and Nintendo on Platform and Role-Playing games such as Mario and Pokémon.

Each dot is one of 10,417 organisations listed on GameDevMap. Dots are placed near city centres, so their positions are approximate.

A quarter are in the United States, and the busiest cities are London, Tokyo and Stockholm. Malaysia has 32 listings, centred on the Klang Valley, where MDEC runs Level Up KL, Southeast Asia's largest game developer conference.

The dot map counts organisations. This map counts the games they produce, as listed on GameCompanies. The USA and Japan lead in both development and publishing.

In most leading countries, publishing listings outnumber development listings, but Canada and Sweden are exceptions, with more development than publishing. Click an activity in the legend to show it on its own.

Where publishers sell their games differs just as much. North America is the largest market for every publisher except Konami.

Western publishers such as Activision sell almost nothing in Japan (2.2%), while Nintendo, Capcom and Konami earn around a quarter of their recorded sales there. Click a publisher in the legend to follow its line.

Section 5

Where Is Gaming Most Widespread?

In every surveyed market, roughly three in four internet users aged 16–64, or more, play video games.

Southeast Asia stands out: the Philippines leads at 96.5%, and Malaysia reaches 88.6%, above the United States and much of Europe. Japan, home to many famous publishers, has the lowest share in the survey. Countries in grey were not surveyed; this does not mean nobody plays there.

Wii Sports recorded more than twice the sales of the next title. It came bundled with the Wii console, so most buyers received it with the machine.

All 15 titles were released between 1984 and 2009. This reflects the period the sales dataset covers, not the market today.

Game over? Far from it

The recorded catalogue has grown several times over, adventure and fantasy have moved to the centre, and players see and share game worlds in new ways. Behind the screen, the industry is concentrated in a few countries and publishers, yet the audience is truly global, and Malaysia is among the most engaged markets.
