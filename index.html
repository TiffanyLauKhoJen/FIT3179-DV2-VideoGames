Inside the Evolution of Video Games

Exploring platforms, genres, publishers and release years across gaming eras

Video games have grown from arcade machines and home consoles into experiences across computers, consoles and smartphones. As technology develops, players can explore imagined worlds, solve puzzles or compete with friends.

From small independent studios to major publishers, game makers shape what reaches players worldwide. Their games become meeting places for friends and competition, making gaming part of everyday life.

Section 1

How Have Video Games Changed Over Time?

Digital stores gave smaller developers more ways to publish games without relying on shop shelves. Affordable development tools and growing mobile audiences also opened opportunities for new creators.

During this period, recorded releases in the IGDB dataset grew from 2,750 in 2010 to 18,108 in 2024—a 6.6-fold increase.

Adventure’s share nearly doubled, rising from 20.9% in 2010 to 38.4% in 2024—the largest increase among the selected genres. Action remained the most common, accounting for 40.2% of recorded main-game releases in 2024. Action games range from Elden Ring’s open‑world combat to Hades’ repeated escape challenges.

Puzzle showed the largest decrease in share, falling from 16.8% to 9.8%. Its recorded game count still increased, but the overall collection grew faster.

Horror rose from fourth to first, overtaking Fantasy in 2023. Recorded releases grew from 335 to 1,433 by 2024. Shorter horror games can suit small development teams, while streamers’ reactions help them reach wider audiences.

Erotic made the largest climb, moving from seventh to third. Its rise coincided with Steam’s broader content policy and adult-content filters introduced in 2018. Meanwhile, Science fiction fell from second to fourth, despite recorded releases increasing from 546 to 734.

Section 2

How Are Video Games Designed to Be Played?

First person showed the largest increase, from 16.7% to 29.1% and overtaking side view and third person. Third person experienced the largest decline in share, decreasing from 28.7% to 17.1%.

Virtual reality reached 4.3% in 2024. Consumer headsets such as the Oculus Rift, launched in 2016, gave developers a platform for games that let players look around and interact through physical movement.

80% of multiplayer-capable games also support single player, giving players the choice to play alone or with others. Sackboy: A Big Adventure, for example, offers solo adventures and co-operative play with up to four players.

Co-operative play appears in 9,929 games, compared with 3,053 offering split screen. It Takes Two builds its adventure around two players helping each other overcome challenges, either on the couch or online.

Section 3

How Do Game Libraries Differ Across Platforms?

Computer accounts for nearly 60% of the selected platform listings, with 115,410 games recorded for Windows alone. Computer storefronts allow small teams to publish alongside major studios. Steam Direct, for example, provides a route to release games with a US$100 submission fee per title.

Nintendo Switch has the largest recorded console catalogue, with 14,273 games, compared with 9,316 on PlayStation 4. Independent developers contribute many titles to its library. Nintendo promotes these games through Indie World, helping smaller studios reach Switch players.

Section 4

Where Is the Game Industry Concentrated?

The United States has the largest concentration of game organisations on the map, with 2,630—around one quarter of the total. Technology and entertainment industries helped hubs develop around Los Angeles, San Francisco and Seattle, providing expertise in programming, animation and storytelling.

London and Tokyo are prominent city hubs, with 279 and 278 game organisations respectively. In the United Kingdom, game clusters benefit from nearby creative businesses, specialist university courses and skilled workers. Japan’s established arcade and console industries provided electronics expertise that helped its game industry develop.

The United States and Japan lead with 18,137 and 12,622 total listings respectively. American publishers such as Electronic Arts and Activision connect development studios with funding, marketing and distribution. In Japan, Nintendo and Sony created global markets for console games, supporting local developers and publishers.

England records Europe’s highest total, with 6,625 listings. Britain’s industry grew from enthusiasts programming games on affordable home computers during the 1980s. Some supplied publishers before founding companies such as Rare and Codemasters, helping turn hobbyist projects into commercial releases.

Section 5

How Do Publishers Differ Across Genres and Markets?

Microsoft’s selected listings lean heavily towards Action, at 59.9%, the largest single-genre share among the four publishers. Konami has a more sporting mix, with 43.7% Sports, led by its Pro Evolution Soccer and Power Pros football and baseball series, released since the 1990s.

For Nintendo, Role-Playing occupies 39.3%, including Pokémon, which has continued across console generations since 1996. Electronic Arts has the strongest Simulator share, at 36.5%, rooted in Maxis, the SimCity developer it acquired in 1997, which later created The Sims.

North America accounts for 51.5% of Activision’s recorded sales. Its Call of Duty: Black Ops led console-game sales revenue there in late 2010. Codemasters records 64.1% in Europe, where Formula One’s large fan base provides an audience for its racing games.

KOEI’s sales are concentrated in Japan (57.5%), while NIS America’s are nearly balanced between North America (38.7%) and Japan (38.5%). NIS America translates and publishes Japanese games for overseas audiences, helping these titles reach international players.

Section 6

How Popular Are Video Games Worldwide?

Southeast Asia leads gaming participation among surveyed markets, with the Philippines at 96.5%, Indonesia at 95.3% and Vietnam at 94.7%. Gaming is also social: people play to connect and compete with friends, an important motivation identified by GlobalWebIndex.

Strong gaming communities help explain this pattern. Niko Partners links the region’s growth to community events, the acceptance of competitive gaming and accessible technology, including smartphones, computers and consoles. Tournaments and livestreams bring players together, giving gaming a place in social life.

Wii Sports leads with 82.74 million recorded copies. Bundling it with Wii consoles in North America and Europe contributed to this total, while simple motion controls made tennis and bowling accessible to newcomers.

Pokémon Red/Blue records 31.37 million copies. Word of mouth and magazine promotion helped early sales grow in Japan. A rare Pokémon Mew giveaway attracted 78,000 entries; its original producer credited the promotion with accelerating sales.

Game over? Far from it

The recorded game catalogue has grown several times over, and players now choose from a wider mix of genres, viewpoints and platforms. Publishers have each carved out their own genre and regional strengths.

The United States and Japan lead recorded development and publishing, while Southeast Asia leads participation among surveyed markets. Gaming’s audience extends beyond its main production centres.
