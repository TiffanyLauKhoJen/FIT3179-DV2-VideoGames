Inside the Evolution of Video Games

Exploring platforms, genres, publishers and release years across gaming eras

Video games have grown from arcade machines and home consoles into experiences across computers, consoles and smartphones. As technology develops, players can explore imagined worlds, solve puzzles or compete with friends.

From small independent studios to major publishers, game makers shape what reaches players worldwide. Their games become meeting places for friends and competition, making gaming part of everyday life.

Section 1

How Have Video Games Changed Over Time?

Digital stores let smaller developers reach players without competing for shop space. Recorded releases grew from 2,750 in 2010 to 18,108 in 2024—a 6.6-fold increase, despite occasional dips along the way.

More releases gave players more games to explore, but made getting noticed harder. Steam, the largest computer game store, added personalised recommendations, so small teams had to find the right audience, not just get listed.

Adventure’s share nearly doubled, rising from 20.9% in 2010 to 38.4% in 2024—the largest increase among the selected genres. Action remained the most common at 40.2%. Elden Ring blends combat with exploration, while Hades pairs fast battles with repeated escape attempts.

Puzzle showed the largest decrease in share, falling from 16.8% to 9.8%. Yet its recorded releases grew from 463 to 1,767; puzzle games became more numerous, but a smaller part of the expanding collection.

Horror rose from fourth to first, overtaking Fantasy in 2023, as releases grew from 335 to 1,433. Independent publishers back small horror teams, and livestreams help their games reach audiences; publisher DreadXP calls streaming an important promotional channel.

Erotic made the largest climb, from seventh to third, after Steam’s 2018 content policy and adult-content filters gave explicit games a route onto its store, hidden unless players opt in. Science fiction fell from second to fourth, although its releases still rose from 546 to 734.

Section 2

How Are Video Games Designed to Be Played?

First person showed the largest increase, from 16.7% to 29.1%. It extends beyond shooters: Cyberpunk 2077’s developers chose it so conversations flow straight into combat without switching camera views.

Third person had the largest decline in share, from 28.7% to 17.1%. It needs more full-body animation and camera work to stop scenery blocking the view, adding work for small teams.

80% of multiplayer-capable games also support single player, giving players the choice to play alone or with others. Sackboy: A Big Adventure, for example, offers solo adventures and co-operative play with up to four players.

Co-operative play appears in 9,929 games, compared with 3,053 offering split screen. The co-operative-only It Takes Two has two players overcome challenges together, either in split screen on the couch or online.

Section 3

How Do Game Libraries Differ Across Platforms?

Computer accounts for about 63% of the selected platform listings, with 115,410 games recorded for Windows alone. Digital storefronts give small teams a route to publish alongside major studios. Steam Direct, for example, charges 100 United States dollars per title, with games reviewed before release.

Nintendo Switch has the largest recorded console catalogue, with 14,273 games, compared with 9,316 on PlayStation 4. Its library extends beyond Nintendo’s own franchises. Unity and Unreal Engine support and Nintendo’s online showcases help independent studios.

Section 4

Where Is the Game Industry Concentrated?

The United States has the most recorded game organisations, with 2,630—around one quarter of the total. Its hubs draw on established industries: Silicon Valley’s computing expertise, Hollywood’s entertainment talent and Seattle’s technology firms built skills and business networks for game production.

London and Tokyo are prominent city hubs, with 293 and 278 organisations respectively. Groups of British studios benefit from nearby creative businesses and specialist education. Tokyo reflects Japan’s long arcade and console history, drawing on electronics expertise and artistic talent from animation and manga.

The United States and Japan lead, with 18,137 and 12,622 development and publishing listings respectively. American publishers such as Electronic Arts support studios through development funding and publishing partnerships. In Japan, Nintendo’s and Sony’s consoles gave local developers access to international audiences.

England has Europe’s highest recorded total, with 6,625 listings. Affordable home computers helped enthusiasts develop programming skills during the 1980s. Codemasters’ founders first supplied games to another publisher before starting their own company, turning home programming into a commercial business.

Section 5

How Do Publishers Differ Across Genres and Markets?

Microsoft has the strongest Action concentration, at 59.9%. Konami’s Sports share reaches 43.7%, reflecting a long history of football and baseball games: its Pro Evolution Soccer predecessors and Power Pros series began in the 1990s.

Role-Playing accounts for 39.3% of Nintendo’s selected titles. Pokémon has carried character collecting and turn-based battles across console generations since 1996. Electronic Arts has the highest Simulator share, at 36.5%. Its 1997 acquisition of Maxis brought SimCity, then The Sims in 2000, extending simulation from cities to everyday lives.

North America accounts for 51.5% of Activision’s recorded sales. Its Call of Duty: Black Ops topped console-game sales revenue there in late 2010. Codemasters records 64.1% in Europe. Electronic Arts highlighted Formula One’s large European fan base when proposing to acquire the racing-game developer.

KOEI’s sales are concentrated in Japan (57.5%), while NIS America’s are nearly balanced between North America (38.7%) and Japan (38.5%). NIS America translates and publishes Japanese games for overseas audiences, connecting their creators with international players.

Section 6

How Popular Are Video Games Worldwide?

Three Southeast Asian markets lead gaming participation, with the Philippines at 96.5%, Indonesia at 95.3% and Vietnam at 94.7% of surveyed internet users aged 16–64. GlobalWebIndex identifies socialising with friends as an important reason people play.

Gaming communities provide further context. Niko Partners highlights Southeast Asia’s strong interest in competitive gaming and community tournaments, which give amateur players chances to compete. Audiences also follow livestreams, linking gaming with social life.

Wii Sports leads with 82.74 million recorded copies. Bundling it with Wii consoles in North America and Europe means the total includes games supplied with hardware. Its simple motion controls let newcomers swing a racket or roll a bowling ball with familiar movements.

Pokémon Red/Blue records 31.37 million copies. Word of mouth and magazine promotion built momentum, and a giveaway for the rare Pokémon Mew drew 78,000 entries in Japan. Producer Tsunekazu Ishihara recalled weekly sales then matching earlier monthly sales.

Game over? Far from it

The recorded game catalogue has grown several times over, and players now choose from a wider mix of genres, viewpoints and platforms. Publishers have each carved out their own genre and regional strengths.

The United States and Japan lead recorded development and publishing, while Southeast Asia leads participation among surveyed markets. Gaming’s audience extends beyond its main production centres.
